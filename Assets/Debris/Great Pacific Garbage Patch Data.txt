GREAT PACIFIC GARBAGE PATCH - VISUALIZATION REFERENCE

BASELINE: Lebreton et al., Scientific Reports (2018), using 2015-2016 surveys.
Historical floating-plastic stock estimate, not a present-day census or annual input.
Study area: approximately 1.6 million square kilometres between Hawaii and California.
Estimated mass: 78,909 metric tonnes (reported range 45,163-128,665).
Estimated number: approximately 1.8 trillion pieces (range 1.1-3.6 trillion).

Size category       Study size range       Approx. pieces     Mass (metric tonnes)
Megaplastic         Greater than 50 cm     3.2 million        42,362
Macroplastic        5-50 cm                821 million        20,195
Mesoplastic         5 mm-5 cm              56 billion         9,971
Microplastic        0.5-5 mm               1.7 trillion       6,380

Mass categories aggregate the published Table 1 rows. Rounded rows sum to
78,908 tonnes; the published overall estimate is 78,909 tonnes. Counts above
use rounded category estimates from the article and should not be summed for
an exact total. Particles below 0.5 mm are outside this baseline's size coverage.

Size categories depend on measured dimensions, not object names. A small buoy
or jerry can is not automatically megaplastic. Fragments can span categories.
The source does not provide patch-wide counts for each of our 17 model types.

NEWER TREND - published November 2024, observations 2015-2022:
Small plastic fragment (0.5-50 mm) mass concentration increased from 2.9 to
14.2 kg per square kilometre. This is a concentration trend, not a new total
mass estimate for the entire patch. Do not multiply the baseline total by it.
https://theoceancleanup.com/updates/persistent-legacy-plastic-fragments-are-rising-disproportionally-faster-than-larger-floating-objects-in-the-great-pacific-garbage-patch/

VISUALIZATION:
The accumulation scene uses the historical 78,909-tonne estimate, 400 markers,
and approximately 197.27 tonnes per marker. Marker sizes, colors and falling
speed are illustrative; colors do not encode the category shares yet.
The total describes accumulated stock, not material dumped during the animation.
The patch is dispersed debris, not a solid island or a literal pile.
